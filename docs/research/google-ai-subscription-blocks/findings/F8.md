## Angle

Angle F8 — COUNTER-ANGLE. Question: how do vendors respond when consumers bypass official clients, and what failure modes do practitioners report? Scope: vendor enforcement announcements, official incident posts, first-hand account-ban reports, OSS relay issue threads/changelogs, token-revocation/expiry events, fingerprint/detection release notes. Vendor docs underrepresent victim/violation evidence; this angle surfaces it. "Consumer subscription" = Gemini app / Google AI Pro / Ultra / Gemini CLI consumer OAuth / Antigravity consumer, distinct from Gemini API keys and Vertex AI. Research is neutral and technical; no exploit code.

Key frame: Google's enforcement is documented chiefly as *policy + incident response*, not as a detection spec. Practitioner artifacts supply the failure-mode detail. All links between specific fingerprint signals and consumer bans remain `[inference]` unless Google says so.

## Conflicting evidence

- **Scope of ban.** Google's maintainer post says Antigravity bans "also blocked access to Gemini CLI and Gemini Code Assist" (claim 1) — collateral across services. Current Antigravity ToS says penalties apply to "Antigravity and/or Gemini CLI accounts" (claim 5). First-hand HN reports describe threats to Gmail/Workspace and whole-account bans (claim 25, implicator.ai coverage). Cannot reconcile: vendor text scopes the penalty, practitioner reports describe broader or feared broader scope. Possibly a change over time (Feb vs current ToS) or distinct enforcement pipelines; **not resolved**.
- **Notification promised vs delivered.** Google promised email + form for flagged accounts from 2026-02-27 (claim 3). Multiple May 2026 reports say no email arrived (claim 23); some February reports say no warning at all (claims 17–18). Either rollout lagged the announcement or the reports predate it. **Not resolved**.
- **Why users were flagged.** Vendor names ToS violations involving third-party tools/proxies (claims 1, 4–6). Practitioners report bans with no third-party use, attributing them to heavy usage, hardware-ID changes, or backend bugs (claims 20, 24). Google has not confirmed false-positive causes. **Not resolved**.
- **What counts as a "third-party tool."** Forum participants note the ToS language is vague enough to implicate ordinary IDE extensions, skills, and agents (thread at claim 13). Google's March 18 post itself drew the same objection from a commenter asking whether `gemini -y -p` in a cron job or Telegram bot violates the ToS (claim 9 thread). **No authoritative boundary published**.
- **Anthropic policy timing.** Secondary sources give three different dates: server-side spoof blocking 2026-01-09; ToS clarification around 2026-02-19/20; further restriction effective 2026-04-04. Only the docs text and the engineer's statement are directly verified; the April 4 date rests on secondary write-ups. **Partially resolved**.
- **API keys vs consumer subscriptions.** Google's leaked-key enforcement and unrestricted-key rejection (claims 11–12) are documented for Gemini API / Cloud keys, not for consumer subscription OAuth. Including them as "detection side adapts" is contextual; conflating them with consumer bans would be wrong. **Scope note, not a conflict**.

## Gaps

- No Google-documented detection latency, rate, threshold, or signal set for consumer-subscription bypass. Every time-to-detection figure is a practitioner observation or a cutover interval.
- No vendor confirmation that User-Agent, binary signature, TLS/JA3/JA4 fingerprint, hardware ID, IP/egress drift, or OAuth client ID is used to flag consumer accounts. All such links flagged `[inference]` / **speculative**.
- No verified count of banned accounts, ban rate, or share of false positives. All "thousands"/"mass" quantifications marked **unverified**.
- Full bodies of several Google One / Wallet support threads not fetched (search snippets only); claim 27 detail is lower confidence.
- No changelog for the Antigravity CLI or Google-side enforcement system documenting specific detection-rule updates; only product-level announcements exist.
- Reason the equal-l2 relay repo was archived (2026-06-05) not documented; timing is suggestive only.
- No primary documentation of token revocation events initiated by Google specifically in response to a detected bypass (as opposed to normal expiry, relay bugs, or endpoint sunset). This is the single largest evidentiary hole in the counter-angle.
