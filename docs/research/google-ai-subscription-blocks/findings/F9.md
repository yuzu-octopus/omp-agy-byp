## Claims

- **C1 — Gemini web app robots.txt, complete raw text.** https://gemini.google.com/robots.txt returned a non-empty, single-group file, exactly: `User-agent: *` / `Allow: /app/download` / `Disallow: /app/` / `Disallow: /chat/` / (blank) / `Sitemap: https://gemini.google.com/sitemap.xml`. Source: https://gemini.google.com/robots.txt — fetched 2026-11-18 — primary. Direct HTTP GET of the raw file; no rendering required.

- **C2 — Scope of the Gemini directives.** One group, `User-agent: *` (all user agents). `/chat/` is Disallowed, `/app/` is Disallowed, `/app/download` is Allowed (explicit carve-out inside the disallowed `/app/` tree), and a sitemap is advertised. The file contains only standard robots.txt path directives (`User-agent`, `Allow`, `Disallow`, `Sitemap`). No AI/training-specific token, no "no train" directive, no purpose-based or activity-based rule appears in the file. Source: https://gemini.google.com/robots.txt — fetched 2026-11-18 — primary.

- **C3 — Google ToS automated-access clause, verbatim and in context.** Effective July 30, 2026; country version United States. Under "What we expect from you" → "Don't abuse our services", preceded by "You must not abuse, harm, interfere with, or disrupt our services or systems — for example, by:", exact bullet: "using automated means to access content from any of our services in violation of the machine-readable instructions on our web pages (for example, robots.txt files that disallow crawling, training, or other activities)". Source: https://policies.google.com/terms — fetched 2026-11-18 — primary. Wording corroborated identically by two independent fetch paths (TinyFish rendered-text extraction and Exa page fetch).

- **C4 — The clause is conditional on the machine-readable instructions, not a blanket automation ban.** Literal structure: the prohibited act is "using automated means to access content ... **in violation of** the machine-readable instructions on our web pages". The parenthetical "(for example, robots.txt files that disallow crawling, training, or other activities)" is an example of such instructions, not a standalone prohibition on all automated access. This is the exact conditional text, not an interpretation of its legal effect. Source: https://policies.google.com/terms — fetched 2026-11-18 — primary.

- **C5 — Textual linkage between the two documents.** The machine-readable instructions the ToS clause conditions on are, for the Gemini web app, the C1 directives. Because `User-agent: *` covers all agents and `Disallow: /chat/` plus `Disallow: /app/` name the web app's chat and app paths, automated access to content on those paths is access "in violation of the machine-readable instructions" as those instructions are written; the `Allow: /app/download` line is the expressed exception within `/app/`. Conversely, the ToS parenthetical's word "training" has no counterpart in the live Gemini robots.txt — the ToS examples are illustrative, while Gemini's actual instructions are path-based. Source pairing: https://gemini.google.com/robots.txt and https://policies.google.com/terms — both fetched 2026-11-18 — primary.

- **C6 — Adjacent ToS bullets (do not conflate with C3).** The same "Don't abuse our services" list separately bans "spamming, hacking, or bypassing our systems or protective measures" and "jailbreaking, adversarial prompting, or prompt injection, except as part of our safety and bug testing programs"; the "Suspending or terminating your access" section independently cites "scraping content that doesn't belong to you". These are distinct clauses, not part of the machine-readable-instructions condition. Source: https://policies.google.com/terms — fetched 2026-11-18 — primary.

- **C7 — Pattern check on a second official Google endpoint.** https://www.google.com/robots.txt uses the same standard vocabulary (`User-agent`, `Allow`, `Disallow`, `Sitemap`, plus comments), with multiple user-agent groups and long path lists; no AI/training-specific token found in that file either. Included only to clarify that Google's machine-readable instructions are conventional robots.txt path rules. Source: https://www.google.com/robots.txt — fetched 2026-11-18 — primary.

## Conflicting evidence

- **F1 C24 vs C3/C4:** F1 recorded a Gemini Apps Help Community thread asserting "Automating the Gemini web app with an AI agent violates Google's Terms of Service, regardless of your query volume or personal subscription plan" (secondary). The primary ToS text is narrower: it conditions the automated-access prohibition on violation of machine-readable instructions (C4). No new primary source was found that states a blanket "all automation is prohibited" rule for the Gemini web app. Treat the community claim as practitioner interpretation.
- **ToS parenthetical "training" vs actual robots.txt (C5):** the ToS examples name "crawling, training, or other activities", but neither Gemini's nor Google's main robots.txt contains a training-specific directive. This is an example-vs-actual-instructions mismatch, not a contradiction; no source was found showing Google uses a dedicated training token in these files.

- No archived or historical version of gemini.google.com/robots.txt was checked; C1/C2 are a snapshot as of 2026-11-18 and could change.
- No official statement found in these sources explaining *why* /chat/ and /app/ are disallowed, or tying the directives to AI-training policy specifically; the files give paths only.
- No primary source found describing how Google detects or enforces automated access that violates these directives (technical enforcement belongs to other angles in the research tree).
- The ToS was fetched as the United States country version; other country versions were not compared for clause wording.
- https://www.google.com/robots.txt is a single comparator, not an exhaustive survey of Google consumer endpoints; absence of a training token there is scoped to that file.
